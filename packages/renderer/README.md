# Procedural motor pool and presentation

Reviewed: 2026-10-25 for `arcade-v8`. *All the squash, none of the collision-authority paperwork.*

`CarFactory` renders the six IDs exported by `@fender/content`'s `VEHICLE_BODIES`: patrol, interceptor, roadster, army, ambulance and fire-chief. Traffic retains its independent civilian body. Geometry/materials and fallback synthesis are original project work; no downloaded models or textures are needed. The [patrol bank](../../spec/AUDIO.md) adds local CC0 recordings and original sound arrangements. Profiles in `@fender/contracts` give the bodies different health/handling; the shared engine recordings receive six distinct pitch/brightness tunings.

## Patrol audio

`pnpm test:audio` runs real renderer/decoding checks and writes a loudness-matched before/after scene to `work/audio/listen.html`, `before.wav` and `after.wav`. Older synthesis tests and captures below remain fallback/historical evidence. Human listening approval is still required.

Graphics are code-authored meshes: `primitives.ts` supplies Babylon box/cylinder/icosphere/plane/torus builders; `city.ts` assembles and batches the district; `cars.ts` adds custom hull vertex rings and instanced details. There are no imported Blender/glTF car or building models. Authored layout, simple color materials, some PBR car paint and cartoon deformation produce the blocky miniature look. See [how the graphics are built](../../spec/ART_AND_ASSETS.md#how-the-graphics-are-built) for the distinction between procedural construction and random generation.

Civilian `traffic-*` bodies omit guns. Player and villain cars retain both physical barrels; AI ownership alone does not imply civilian traffic. The renderer derives that distinction from the body ID, so campaign targets retain their assigned body and weapons when pursuit guidance is absent in briefings/results. Legacy `suspect` keeps its gold body with an armed template separate from civilian gold traffic.

`createGameRenderer(canvas, map?, options?)` exposes `render(frame, dt)`, `present(events)`, `setSettings(options)`, `setDispatch(dispatchId | null)`, `setGaragePreview(bodyAssetId | null)`, `resize`, `loadScene`, `unlockAudio`, `getStats` and `dispose`. The briefing uses the original car meshes in a separately lit shared chase scene. One camera shows the suspect ahead of the selected unit; road markings move backward and the cars sway gently. Responsive viewports leave both stat cards and the briefing clear. Reduced motion freezes decorative motion. A standalone body without a dispatch retains its inspection turntable. Call the garage setter with the selected body during the connected lobby and `null` on entry or during play. Repeated selection is idempotent; a body change on an existing gameplay entity also rebuilds its visual. Protected cars blink through supported instance boolean visibility; reduced flash keeps them solid. Their protection marker remains visible, and wreck silhouettes retain the existing smoke/fire cues. Shared materials and unsupported instance alpha setters are untouched. Health bars use authoritative `maxHealth` with the shared profile as fallback.

## Dispatch atmosphere

`environment.ts` selects authored noon/dusk/night sun or moon direction, key/fill colors, exposure and linear distance fog. The app supplies the current shared campaign dispatch ID in practice and online, including briefing/results; no new network field or client wall clock is involved. Null/unknown dispatches select noon. A custom `loadScene` environment remains the noncampaign baseline; asynchronous loading cannot overwrite an active dispatch. The garage retains its independent studio scene.

Every upright street lamp stays illuminated at dusk/night on every quality preset, regardless of camera distance. `street-light-field.ts` caches their reflected/scaled head positions in one generated 512×512 RGBA texture, rebuilding only when lamp state changes. Standard/PBR materials sample this world-space diffuse field before fog and tone mapping; height and surface direction soften the fill on cars/walls and keep it off roofs above the lamps. This avoids both the former two-nearest-lamp pop-in and a large per-material dynamic-light limit. It adds one shared texture, no lamp meshes/draw passes or shadow lights, and follows the prop adapter's 192-state bound. Removed/fallen lamps lose their illumination; distance never switches them off. Lamp bulbs retain steady warm emission. Existing filtered directional shadows remain on medium/high and are disabled on low. `getStats` reports `environmentTheme`, `environmentDispatchId` and `environmentLights` (illuminating lamp count, currently 30 at night in the district).

Cornering heel and squash/stretch are pure presentation driven by bounded lateral acceleration. Coachwork deforms separately from rigid wheels; the suspension can heave, while health bars and the ground marker stay outside the deformed hierarchy. Six authored striped speed humps (`ROAD_BUMPS`) trigger compression, stretch and a short damped rebound when crossed. They add no authority geometry or collision response. Teleports reset motion; idle, replayed or zero-time samples do not produce new impulses. Reduced shake or the operating system's reduced-motion preference stops garage rotation and resets novelty deformation.

Boing and tire-squeak cues require intentional audio activation, honor mute/volume, are locally triggered and rate limited, and share the existing 12-voice bound. Existing combat events remain deduplicated. The garage suppresses gameplay audio/effects and low quality creates no garage shadow map. Scenes, instances, materials, native audio nodes and bounded effect pools follow the renderer's disposal lifecycle.

Confirmed `crash` events drive a directional coachwork spring using the authoritative normal and closing speed. Frontal/rear impacts squash length and pitch in opposite directions; side impacts squash width and roll. The short damped overshoot composes with cornering and humps, then settles. Authority pose, rigid wheels and health/marker transforms are unchanged. Discontinuities/respawn reset the spring; old-life events, repeated IDs, retired matches and contact bursts are rejected. Entering the garage starts a fresh presentation session so returning to an existing room after playing elsewhere remains valid. Motion reduction suppresses impact/recoil and camera shake.

Confirmed shots add bounded coachwork recoil and paired faceted muzzle flashes at the two physical barrel tips. Current volley events supply two exact origins; the legacy center-origin fallback alone adds lateral gun spacing. Tracers combine white-hot cores and warm additive trails; hits, authoritative wall strikes and wrecks add pooled flash/ring/spark/smoke/fire effects. Flash freshness and spring peak sampling guarantee an initial visible frame even at 10 FPS. Fixed pools contain 24 flash slots, 12 rings, 192 core/trail pairs and the existing 96 particles. Shared native geometry/materials require no light or material allocation per shot; disposal removes all instances, sources and materials.

Crash audio is full strength locally and fades remote impacts to silence at 35 metres. Other combat effects fade to silence by 45 metres; the local driver's gun retains full feedback. Sound severity and distance remain independent. Confirmed event sounds share match/event deduplication; garage scenes suppress them. `getStats` also reports live muzzle flashes, impact rings and tracer counts.

## Pooled cartoon wreck sequence

A confirmed wreck replaces intact coachwork and road wheels with a shared charred chassis. `CarFactory.breakupParts(body, traffic)` retains every original primitive/custom hull before intact material batching, with a centered mesh, component ID and original material. Eight reusable visual slots switch to that exact geometry: 40 civilian components, 47 patrol components, up to 58 ambulance components. Wheels, hubs, caps, trim, mirrors, lights and permitted guns separate independently; no generic six-panel substitute is used. Fireballs, shock rings and smoke accompany the breakup. Everything clears by 2.6 seconds or an earlier lifecycle reset. A wrecked snapshot alone shows the chassis without replaying an explosion.

`WreckEffects(scene, factory?, map?)` exposes `ready`, `spawn`, `update`, `remove`, `reset`, `getStats` and `dispose`. The live renderer supplies its existing factory/map; standalone effects can own a factory. Descriptor fields remain `vehicleId`, `pose`, `bodyAssetId` and `discontinuityId`. Visual meshes are pooled and reuse original geometry/materials. Each active piece gets a Rapier rigid body with a conservative cuboid collider in a separate client-only world: gravity, spin, damping, CCD, ground/building contact and piece-to-piece collisions. A 160 ms separation interval avoids overlap impulses from the intact assembly before fragment contacts activate. The fixed 60 Hz visual world admits at most six steps per update and at most 464 active bodies across eight full ambulance breakups. Body/collider allocation occurs at breakup and is released on cleanup; no claim of zero per-wreck physics allocation is made.

`@fender/physics` supplies one shared WASM initialization promise for simulation and debris; the worlds stay independent. `loadScene()` waits for debris readiness. An early wreck queues its initial assembly until ready; live reduced-motion changes suppress it immediately, including during initialization. `getStats()` adds `wreckPieces`, `wreckBodies`, `wreckPartCollisions` and `wreckPhysicsReady`. Debris has no authority, car/pedestrian contact, damage or network state. Reduced motion removes its rigid bodies; reduced flash removes hot cores/rings and limits fire puffs.

The renderer accepts complete authority metadata even when the corresponding snapshot still says active. A life mismatch rejects a delayed event; a legacy wreck event without life metadata is accepted only for an already-wrecked snapshot. Event IDs and per-life guards prevent repeated blasts/audio. Teleport, respawn, entity leave, garage entry, match change and disposal clean up the wreck pool. The motor-pool `wreckParts` remain outside deformation and use supported boolean instance visibility. Health/identity transforms and authority pose are untouched.

Wreck audio is an original six-layer bass blast, debris clatter, falling whistle and rubbery coda within the existing 12-source and .055-per-source-gain limits. Distance, current volume and mute apply to every layer. `effect('wreck', volumeScale, ownerId?)` retains the original effect API; `stopWreck(ownerId?)` cancels a vehicle's tail or all wreck tails during lifecycle cleanup while preserving unrelated shots.

## Physical twin-barrel volleys

Both modeled guns end at local forward 2.45 metres and lateral +/-0.63 metres, with neutral world height 1.20 metres. A current `shot` event supplies `origins` and `projectileIds` for its two real rounds, plus one `volleyId`. One event produces one sound/recoil response and two flashes; volley ID deduplication also handles replayed events with different envelope IDs. Legacy center-origin events retain their previous paired-flash fallback.

Flash requests wait until the next render has updated the car and coachwork matrices. For a known shooter, flash centers come from the actual visible gun-tip transform, including cornering heel, suspension and recoil; the event's assisted trajectory yaw is retained. During the short flash lifetime the flashes continue to follow those mounts. When the shooter is not drawn, the exact provided origins are used once each with no extra lateral offset. All requests/bindings remain bounded and are cleared with the existing match/session lifecycle.

Each actual projectile renders one core/trail pair from the supplied presentation pose; the renderer does not rebase or mutate projectile states. Core/trail length is limited by both render-only `presentationAgeSeconds` and first-visible history so a fresh trace cannot extend backwards through its birth muzzle. The renderer uses no assumption that `sampleTimeSeconds` is a server tick. The fixed 192-pair pool exceeds the nominal eight-driver load at the default 0.65-second projectile lifetime and is stress-tested with 240 inputs.
