# Procedural motor pool and presentation

`CarFactory` renders the six IDs exported by `@fender/content`'s `VEHICLE_BODIES`: patrol, interceptor, roadster, army, ambulance and fire-chief. Traffic retains its independent civilian body. Geometry, materials and synthesized sounds are original project-authored work; no downloaded models or textures are needed. Profiles in `@fender/contracts` give these vehicles different health and handling. Footprints and weapons remain shared.

`createGameRenderer(canvas, map?, options?)` exposes `render(frame, dt)`, `present(events)`, `setSettings(options)`, `setGaragePreview(bodyAssetId | null)`, `resize`, `loadScene`, `unlockAudio`, `getStats` and `dispose`. The garage uses the original car meshes on its own lit turntable scene in the left 58 percent of the canvas. Call the setter with the selected body on home/online lobby screens and `null` during play. Repeated selection is idempotent; a body change on an existing gameplay entity also rebuilds its visual. Protected cars blink through supported instance boolean visibility; reduced flash keeps them solid. Their protection marker remains visible, and wreck silhouettes retain the existing smoke/fire cues. Shared materials and unsupported instance alpha setters are untouched. Health bars use authoritative `maxHealth` with the shared profile as fallback.

Cornering heel and squash/stretch are pure presentation driven by bounded lateral acceleration. Coachwork deforms separately from rigid wheels; the suspension can heave, while health bars and the ground marker stay outside the deformed hierarchy. Six authored striped speed humps (`ROAD_BUMPS`) trigger compression, stretch and a short damped rebound when crossed. They add no authority geometry or collision response. Teleports reset motion; idle, replayed or zero-time samples do not produce new impulses. Reduced shake or the operating system's reduced-motion preference stops garage rotation and resets novelty deformation.

Boing and tire-squeak cues require intentional audio activation, honor mute/volume, are locally triggered and rate limited, and share the existing 12-voice bound. Existing combat events remain deduplicated. The garage suppresses gameplay audio/effects and low quality creates no garage shadow map. Scenes, instances, materials, native audio nodes and bounded effect pools follow the renderer's disposal lifecycle.

Confirmed `crash` events drive a directional coachwork spring using the authoritative normal and closing speed. Frontal/rear impacts squash length and pitch in opposite directions; side impacts squash width and roll. The short damped overshoot composes with cornering and humps, then settles. Authority pose, rigid wheels and health/marker transforms are unchanged. Discontinuities/respawn reset the spring; old-life events, repeated IDs, retired matches and contact bursts are rejected. Entering the garage starts a fresh presentation session so returning to an existing room after playing elsewhere remains valid. Motion reduction suppresses impact/recoil and camera shake.

Confirmed shots add bounded coachwork recoil and paired faceted muzzle flashes at the two physical barrel tips. Current volley events supply two exact origins; the legacy center-origin fallback alone adds lateral gun spacing. Tracers combine white-hot cores and warm additive trails; hits, authoritative wall strikes and wrecks add pooled flash/ring/spark/smoke/fire effects. Flash freshness and spring peak sampling guarantee an initial visible frame even at 10 FPS. Fixed pools contain 24 flash slots, 12 rings, 192 core/trail pairs and the existing 96 particles. Shared native geometry/materials require no light or material allocation per shot; disposal removes all instances, sources and materials.

Crash audio is full strength locally and fades remote impacts to silence at 35 metres. Other combat effects fade to silence by 45 metres; the local driver's gun retains full feedback. Sound severity and distance remain independent. Confirmed event sounds share match/event deduplication; garage scenes suppress them. `getStats` also reports live muzzle flashes, impact rings and tracer counts.

## Pooled cartoon wreck sequence

A confirmed wreck replaces intact coachwork and road wheels with a shared charred chassis. Eight reusable sequences each launch four recognizable tire/hub/cap assemblies and six body-colored parts: hood, two doors with windows/handles, roof, bumper and boot. Pieces spin, tumble, bounce and shrink away; orange/gold fireballs, two shock rings and rising smoke run alongside them. Every sequence is fully hidden by 2.6 seconds, before the normal three-second respawn. Debris has no authoritative physics or damage. A wrecked snapshot alone shows the chassis without replaying the explosion, including late joins.

`WreckEffects(scene)` exposes `spawn(descriptor, options)`, `update(dt, options)`, `remove(vehicleId)`, `reset`, `getStats` and `dispose`. Descriptor fields are `vehicleId`, `pose`, `bodyAssetId` and `discontinuityId`; options are `reducedMotion` and `reducedFlash`. All meshes and shared materials are prepared in bounded pools, with no lights, textures or downloaded assets. Debris colors follow the original vehicle paint, including the separate civilian traffic body. Reduced motion suppresses all flying/tumbling pieces; reduced flash removes the white-hot core and rings and limits visible fire puffs.

The renderer accepts complete authority metadata even when the corresponding snapshot still says active. A life mismatch rejects a delayed event; a legacy wreck event without life metadata is accepted only for an already-wrecked snapshot. Event IDs and per-life guards prevent repeated blasts/audio. Teleport, respawn, entity leave, garage entry, match change and disposal clean up the wreck pool. The motor-pool `wreckParts` remain outside deformation and use supported boolean instance visibility. Health/identity transforms and authority pose are untouched.

Wreck audio is an original six-layer bass blast, debris clatter, falling whistle and rubbery coda within the existing 12-source and .055-per-source-gain limits. Distance, current volume and mute apply to every layer. `effect('wreck', volumeScale, ownerId?)` retains the original effect API; `stopWreck(ownerId?)` cancels a vehicle's tail or all wreck tails during lifecycle cleanup while preserving unrelated shots.

## Physical twin-barrel volleys

Both modeled guns end at local forward 2.45 metres and lateral +/-0.63 metres, with neutral world height 1.20 metres. A current `shot` event supplies `origins` and `projectileIds` for its two real rounds, plus one `volleyId`. One event produces one sound/recoil response and two flashes; volley ID deduplication also handles replayed events with different envelope IDs. Legacy center-origin events retain their previous paired-flash fallback.

Flash requests wait until the next render has updated the car and coachwork matrices. For a known shooter, flash centers come from the actual visible gun-tip transform, including cornering heel, suspension and recoil; the event's assisted trajectory yaw is retained. During the short flash lifetime the flashes continue to follow those mounts. When the shooter is not drawn, the exact provided origins are used once each with no extra lateral offset. All requests/bindings remain bounded and are cleared with the existing match/session lifecycle.

Each actual projectile renders one core/trail pair from the supplied presentation pose; the renderer does not rebase or mutate projectile states. Core/trail length is limited by both render-only `presentationAgeSeconds` and first-visible history so a fresh trace cannot extend backwards through its birth muzzle. The renderer uses no assumption that `sampleTimeSeconds` is a server tick. The fixed 192-pair pool exceeds the nominal eight-driver load at the default 0.65-second projectile lifetime and is stress-tested with 240 inputs.
