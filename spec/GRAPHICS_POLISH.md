## Quality and accessibility contract

| Feature | Low | Medium | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Theme, sky, ambient and distance fog | Full authored palette; readable roads | Same | Same |
| Directional shadows | Disabled | Existing filtered directional map | Existing filtered directional map; refine only with evidence |
| Street illumination | Every upright lamp; shared diffuse field and emissive bulbs | Same | Same |
| Wet surfaces | Static small-wave finish; one shared 128 × 128 reflection, refreshed every other frame | Slow crossing ripples/creases; one shared 256 × 256 scene reflection | Same bounded reflection and ripples |
| Source glints/bloom | Disabled | Up to eight steady source halos, with building occlusion | Same; source-local soft glow, no full-screen bloom pass |
| Dynamic car headlights | Disabled | One shadowless cone | Two shadowless cones |

Reduced flash suppresses flares/glints and bloom pulses; fixed ambient light and steady lamp illumination stay available. Reduced motion freezes decorative water animation and removes unnecessary lighting transitions. No strobing street lights, exposure pumping, compulsory lens dirt, depth-of-field blur or motion blur. The garage retains its separate studio lighting.

Every upright lamp contributes to one cached 512×512 diffuse-light texture, sampled by Standard/PBR surfaces before fog and tone mapping. Lamp state comes from actual prop poses; camera movement and quality changes never select a subset or switch lamps off. Fallen/removed lamps lose their contribution. The former two/one/zero spotlight limits were rejected by the owner on 2026-10-25 and are superseded by this requirement. The shared field adds no per-lamp shadow maps or render passes. Its approximate diffuse fill is not a new specular reflection or occlusion system. Dynamic shadow maps, full-screen effects and reflections must be measured together with 40–58-piece wrecks, rather than receiving independent imaginary frame budgets.

## Delivered rendering slice

The authored water inventory contains four road puddles, one park pond and one garden pool. The pond and pool use flat pale borders to communicate shallow ornamental water. The original graphics slice made all six cosmetic. The tutorial build adds shared road-puddle polygons and 75% lateral grip from dispatch 4; ponds and pools remain decorative and no water collider is added. Conservative footprint tests check buildings, spawn clearance, pedestrian loops and both dirt shortcuts; the native check writes a matching map overlay. One shared opaque PBR water material combines captured nearby scenery, view-dependent Fresnel sheen, crossing small-wave normals, narrow creases and a lighter shallow edge. A single target is shared by visible sites, omits water itself, and is removed from the render schedule when no water is visible. Pond/pool use their shared height; road puddles share this mirror plane when both elevations are visible (a 25 cm approximation). Low retains static ripples with a 128 × 128 alternate-frame reflection; medium/high use 256 × 256 each frame. Reduced motion freezes the ripple phase immediately while reflections still track the real camera and scene. Synthetic sky bands are superseded; earlier captures retain their original finish.

Cars now throw pale airborne tire fans when their leading tires sweep an authored water footprint, including fast between-frame and reverse crossings. Spray strength follows speed; initial appearances, idle/crawling cars, wrecks, life changes and teleports do not create bursts. A fixed shared-geometry pool caps spray at 96 drops (48 on low). Reduced motion suppresses airborne spray. An original short noise wash/bubble-tail sound follows the same crossing cue, is distance attenuated, rate-limited per car and obeys master/effects volume, mute and the existing twelve-voice cap. No grip, collision or wire changes accompany this effect. Acceptance at the normal driving camera: a 14 m/s crossing produces two visible tire fans, more than 100 changed spray pixels, droplets above 0.6 m, one short cue, clean garage/leave/life cleanup and no shader/page errors. `water-splash-check.ts` records the native pixels and an audible, unclipped OfflineAudioContext sample.

Night windows emit steady warm light. Car headlight cones follow the actual rendered body transform, and their separate 0/1/2 low/medium/high budget does not limit street lamps. Eight shared source-halo planes add restrained local glow. Camera frustum clipping and conservative building/roof tests remove hidden sources; reduced flash removes halos immediately on the next frame. Garage entry, wreck/respawn, missing entities and disposal clear the corresponding effects. The district retains its one existing shadow-casting directional light.

Coated paint, dielectric glass and chrome use distinct responses; road, dirt and curb have restrained specular differences. Thirty-two shared-texture contact patches anchor present vehicles to the authored road/dirt/grass elevations. Existing filtered directional shadows remain in place. Bevels and screen-space AO remain disabled. Water alone uses the bounded shared planar reflection described above; car glass never samples it. The borough does not issue each effect its own imaginary GPU.

## Reproduce and record

Start `pnpm exec tsx packages/renderer/scenarios/server.ts`, then run `pnpm exec tsx packages/renderer/scenarios/environment-check.ts work/persistent-street-lights`. The native fixture writes same-camera noon/dusk/night captures and JSON evidence including browser version, quality, scene statistics and visible-road pixel checks. Use installed Playwright Chromium, or `CHROME_PATH` for a system Chromium executable. Inspect actual gameplay distance before any optional close-up.

Acceptance for the first slice: retain chunky car/building silhouettes; noon is fresh, dusk is warm with cool fill, night is blue with visible roads and localized warm lamp pools. Distant haze adds depth without hiding nearby intersections. Confirm zero page errors, bounded resource counts after repeated dispatch/quality changes, no lights for absent/fallen lamps, and full disposal. Fixture captures establish that controlled scene only; human visual approval remains open; hardware timing qualification is retired.

To inspect the new slice in an isolated worktree, start the native server with `RENDER_SCENARIO_PORT=5185`, then set `RENDER_SCENARIO_URL=http://127.0.0.1:5185` when running `pnpm exec tsx packages/renderer/scenarios/polish-check.ts work/graphics-polish`. Environment-variable assignment syntax depends on the shell; in PowerShell use `$env:NAME='value'`. The check records dry/wet noon/dusk/night captures, actual water pixel differences, moving-camera frames, source occlusion, accessibility and ten garage/leave resource cycles. `water-appearance.json` measures actual pond pixels: visible sky-color response (>6 average RGB levels), broad highlight contrast (>8 luminance levels), movement (>0.8 average RGB levels over two seconds), and exactly frozen low/reduced-motion output. These regression bounds supplement owner visual review. `surface-before.png` reproduces the previous paint/glass settings at the same camera; it is not a capture of a separately deployed build.

`pnpm exec tsx packages/renderer/scenarios/polish-app-check.ts work/graphics-polish-app` starts dedicated servers on ports 2587/5193 and checks actual solo entry, two-browser dispatch theme changes, a dropped-socket reconnect and ten real UI/network rematches in the unified room model. Two retries warm caches before mesh counts and garbage-collected browser heap are sampled; each sample is saved immediately. These measurements do not report GPU allocation bytes. Dispatch indices are staged through authority checkpoints; this does not replace a human campaign playthrough. `pnpm exec playwright test --config packages/renderer/scenarios/playwright.config.ts` runs the existing app suite on separate ports 2589/5195. The current build uses `arcade-v26` / protocol 5; earlier fixture evidence retains its original version and scope.

`pnpm exec tsx packages/renderer/scenarios/polish-performance.ts work/graphics-polish-performance` bundles the action fixture for production, serves it on port 5194, warms it for fifteen seconds and records five minutes at medium/1920×1080. It writes exact device/browser metadata, frame interval/CPU/GPU percentiles, draw calls, transferred bytes and component/effect maxima. `POLISH_TRACE_QUALITY=low` selects 1280×720 low as a diagnostic preset, not a minimum-device test. `POLISH_TRACE_SECONDS` may shorten diagnostic runs; neither duration nor frame percentiles qualify hardware or block graphics completion. This fixture measures eight-player-equivalent rendering with eight traffic cars, not server authority, network bandwidth or the complete production HUD. Functional app/network and memory checks remain separate.

Set `POLISH_CPU_PROFILE=1` to save a Chrome sampling profile as `cpu.cpuprofile`; profiler runs are diagnostic. `POLISH_HEADLIGHTS=off` is a fixture-only cost ablation: it excludes the car cones from all receiver shaders while retaining the same quality, source halos, lamp field, water, geometry and physics. Evidence labels that mode and asserts zero headlight receivers. It is a cost comparison, not an acceptance run. Clear that variable or set it to `on` for normal rendering. For useful comparisons, run sequentially on the same device without other render/test workloads. No particular device is required.
